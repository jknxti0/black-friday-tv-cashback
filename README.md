## How to tell a real Black Friday TV deal from a fake one

Two checks catch most of the fakery, and neither takes more than a minute.

First, ignore the percentage on the ad. Retailers set reference prices that make discounts look bigger than they are. Instead, look up the model's price history: CamelCamelCamel and Keepa track Amazon prices, and Google Shopping shows historical pricing at other retailers. A genuine Black Friday deal beats the lowest price of the past 90 days by at least 15%. If it doesn't, you're looking at a normal price with a big red sticker.

Second, check the exact model number against what you researched. Brands manufacture cheap derivative versions of popular TVs specifically for Black Friday — a "55-inch Samsung" at a dramatic discount is sometimes a lower-spec panel that shares almost nothing with the model you read reviews for. Write down the full model number (the letter-and-digit string, not just the series name) before the sale starts, and match it character for character at checkout.

One more retailer quirk: neither Walmart nor Amazon price-matches competitors, but Walmart will match a lower price you find on its own website if you're shopping in-store. Worth checking your phone before you pay at the register.
